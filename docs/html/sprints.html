00Strategy

Vertical slices, riskiest first. The reason is RISK-001: the entire project rests on whether the timing feels right, and no amount of architecture makes that question answerable from a desk. So the hardware spike comes before the repo, and the child's real week of use comes before the adult's features.

Story IDs are US-<sprint>.<nn>. Each story names the FR it delivers and the TA it builds against — both are clickable throughout this page. Estimates are in points where 1 point ≈ half a focused day.

Definition of ReadyThe story names its FR and TA references, its acceptance criteria are testable without a human judgement call, and its test scenarios already exist in 04-TEST-SCENARIOS.md. A story that fails any of the three does not enter a sprint.

Definition of Done is the nine-point list in 05-ENGINEERING-HANDBOOK.md, and it includes the documentation clause — see CLAUDE.md for exactly which files each kind of change obliges you to touch.

S0Hardware spike

Sprint 0Hardware spike1–2 days · 6 pts

The only sprint with a go/no-go gate. Nothing else is built until this is answered. One HTML file, no framework, no repo structure.

Goal: know the real latency of the real instrument on the real laptop.

Ready to runThe spike is already built: open sprint-0-spike.html at the repo root. It measures everything above, renders the go/no-go verdict itself, and emits a pre-filled docs/sprint-0-findings.md.

Exit criteria

  • Measured latency is known and recorded in the repo.
  • The instrument's MIDI quirks — velocity-0 note-off, active sensing rate, CC behaviour — are documented.
  • Go/no-go: if latency after calibration is above ~80 ms and cannot be reduced, stop and reconsider before building anything.
